Freeze Reversi

Support and help · for macOS

Reversi, with one addition: each player holds two freeze discs that can lock a counter's color for the rest of the game. This page explains how the game works and how to get help.

Getting help

Questions, problems, or a bug to report — write to:

freeze.reversi@icloud.com

Please say which version of macOS you are running and which variant and level you were playing. If the game behaved differently from what you expected, the move list matters more than anything else, so describe the position if you can.

The game

Players take turns placing a disc of their color. A move is legal only if it traps one or more of the opponent's discs in a straight line between the disc you place and another of your own; every trapped disc turns over. If you have no legal move you pass. When neither player can move, whoever has more discs wins.

Freeze discs

Each player starts with two freeze discs. Press ⌘F, or click Freeze This Move, before you place a disc. The disc you place next is frozen: it wears a brass ring and its color is fixed for the rest of the game.

A frozen disc still behaves normally in every other respect. It can form the far end of a line that captures, and discs between it and another of your discs still turn over. It simply never turns over itself. One restriction follows from this: a move must still turn over at least one disc that is not frozen, or it is not a legal move.

A freeze disc is worth more held than spent. The computer prices an unspent token highly and generally keeps its freezes into the middle game, looking for a square that anchors a line it wants to keep. Spending both early is usually a mistake.

Variants

Choose one in Settings (⌘,) or from the Game menu. Changing the variant starts a new game.

Standard
Reversi on the usual 8×8 board.
Freeze Discs
8×8, two freeze discs each.
6×6 Quick
A smaller board and a shorter game, about ten minutes.
Anti-Reversi
Fewest discs wins. Empty squares go to the winner. The computer plays this one casually rather than well.
6×6 with Freeze
The small board with freeze discs, where each frozen disc controls a larger share of the lines.

Difficulty

Five levels, set in Settings or from the Game menu. Strength comes mainly from how many moves ahead the computer looks; the time limit only caps how long you wait for it.

The 6×6 board uses its own, shallower ladder, because a move reaches proportionally further on a smaller board. Settings shows the figures for whichever variant is selected.

Questions that come up

The computer is taking too long.

Lower the time limit in Settings, or press ⌘. to make it play its best move so far. The computer always has a sound move ready, so interrupting it never produces a bad one — only a slightly less considered one.

Can I play another person?

Yes, on the same Mac. Set Opponent to Two Players in Settings.

Why can't I play on that square?

A move must turn over at least one disc. In the freeze variants it must turn over at least one disc that is not frozen. If the dots marking legal moves are hidden, turn Show my legal moves back on in Settings.

My game was still there when I reopened the app.

That is deliberate. The game in progress is saved when you quit and restored when you return. ⌘N starts a fresh one.

Does the game collect anything about me?

No. See the privacy policy.
